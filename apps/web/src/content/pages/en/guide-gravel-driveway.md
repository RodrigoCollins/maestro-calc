# How much gravel for a driveway?

A gravel driveway is a few layers of gravel, each spread and compacted, shaped so water runs off it. How much you order comes from the size of the driveway and the depth of each layer; how long it lasts comes mostly from the gravel on top and from keeping water off it.

## Plan the layers

Build the driveway in layers and compact each one before the next. A layer has to be at least twice as thick as the largest stone in it, the rule the Federal Highway Administration gives for gravel roads: with 1 in stone, a layer of at least 2 in.

Work out each layer apart, with its own depth, and add the results. The gravel calculator does one layer at a time.

## Work out the tons

Say the driveway is 60 ft long and 10 ft wide, with a top layer 4 in deep, compacted, and 5 percent extra:

- 4 in is 1/3 ft, so the layer fills 60 × 10 × 1/3 = 200 cubic feet in place, or 7.41 cubic yards.
- With 5 percent extra, that is 210 cubic feet, or 7.78 cubic yards.
- Compacted gravel weighs approximately 1.83 tons per cubic yard, so the layer takes 7.78 × 1.83 = 14.23 tons.

If the base under it is another 4 in layer of the same size, it takes the same 14.23 tons again: 28.46 tons for both.

## Order the right amount

Order by weight when the yard sells by the ton. If it sells by the cubic yard, order the loose volume, not the volume in place: loose gravel weighs approximately 1.29 tons per cubic yard, so the 14.23 tons of the top layer are 14.23 ÷ 1.29 = 11.03 cubic yards loose. Ordering the 7.78 cubic yards in place would come up short, because gravel takes less room once it is compacted.

Ask the yard how much a truck carries and whether there is a minimum, and where the truck can dump so you can spread it.

## Choose the gravel for the top

The gravel you drive on is not the same as clean stone. Good surface gravel is a blend of crushed stone, sand and fine material that binds it together, so it packs into a crust and resists washboarding. Clean stone with no fines drains well, but nothing binds it, so it stays loose. Ask the yard for gravel made for driveways or roads, with fines.

## Keep the water off

Water is what ruins a gravel driveway: it softens the gravel and turns low spots into potholes. Give the surface a crown, a straight slope from the center to each edge. The Federal Highway Administration recommends about 4 percent for gravel roads, about 1/2 in per foot. On a driveway 10 ft wide crowned in the middle, each side is 5 ft, so the center sits 5 × 1/2 = 2 1/2 in above the edges.

Where the ground stays wet and soft, a fabric laid on the soil before the first layer keeps the mud from working up into the gravel. Water should still have somewhere to go along the sides.
