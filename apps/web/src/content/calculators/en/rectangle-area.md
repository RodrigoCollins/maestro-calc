<h2 id="how-to-use">How to use the square footage calculator</h2>

The calculator turns the length and the width of a floor, a wall or any other rectangle into its area. The result updates as you type, so there is nothing to submit.

1. Measure the length and the width of the surface with a tape.
2. Enter the two measurements. Type them the way you read a tape: 12 ft, 12 ft 6 in or 12 ft 6 1/2 in. A plain number is read in feet. You can also write the unit yourself, for example 150 in or 4 yd.
3. Read the area. The result shows it in square feet, square yards and square meters, each rounded to two decimal places.

To work in metric, press Metric (m, cm). A plain number is then read in meters, and you can type centimeters as well, for example 3.5 m or 350 cm. The result then leads with square meters. Switching units never changes your numbers, only how they are written.

Use Copy link to send your exact numbers to a partner, a client or a supplier, and the Español link to open the same numbers in Spanish.

<h2 id="formulas">Formulas and worked example</h2>

The area of a rectangle is its length times its width:

- Area in square feet = length in feet × width in feet.
- Area in square yards = area in square feet ÷ 9, because a square yard is 3 ft × 3 ft.
- Area in square meters = area in square feet × 0.09290304.

The last factor is exact. A foot is defined as exactly 0.3048 meters, so a square foot is 0.3048 × 0.3048 = 0.09290304 square meters. Inches count as twelfths of a foot, so 6 in is 0.5 ft and 12 ft 6 in is 12.5 ft.

Worked example: a room 12 ft long and 10 ft wide.

- The area is 12 ft × 10 ft = 120.00 square feet.
- In square yards, 120 ÷ 9 = 13.33 square yards.
- In square meters, 120 × 0.09290304 = 11.1483648, which rounds to 11.15 square meters.

If the same room were 12 ft 6 in long, the area would be 12.5 × 10 = 125.00 square feet, which is 13.89 square yards and 11.61 square meters.

The calculator keeps exact fractions from the first input to the last step, and it rounds the figures only when it shows them. The page [How we calculate](/methodology#square-footage) lists the formulas and the cases we test.

<h2 id="tips">Tips and common mistakes</h2>

Measure wall to wall at floor level, and measure each side in two or three places. Rooms are rarely perfectly square, so use the longest measurement of each side. Round up to the next inch rather than down.

Check the unit of every number before you read the answer. The most common mistake is typing inches where feet are expected: a plain 150 is read as 150 feet, not 150 inches. Write the unit yourself, for example 150 in or 12 ft 6 in, and the calculator reads exactly what you typed.

Do not multiply the two sides in decimal feet by hand from a tape that reads feet and inches. 12 ft 6 in is 12.5 ft, not 12.6 ft. Type the measurement as you read it and let the calculator convert it.

For a room that is not a rectangle, such as an L-shaped room, split the floor into rectangles that do not overlap. Run the calculator once for each rectangle and add the results. For a closet or an alcove that gets the same floor, measure it as its own rectangle and add it.

Leave out the areas that the material will not cover. If the flooring stops at the base of a kitchen island or built-in cabinets, subtract their footprint. For a wall, measure the width and the height of the wall, then subtract each door and window as its own rectangle.

The calculator gives the area you measured, with nothing added for waste. Flooring, tile and sheet goods lose material to cuts, and installers commonly add about 10 percent for a simple layout and more for a diagonal or patterned one. Check what the manufacturer of your product and your installer recommend.

Materials are sold by the box, the roll or the sheet, each covering a stated area. Divide the area you need by the coverage of one unit and round up to a whole unit.

The figure is the area of the surface you measured. It is not the same as the finished living area on a listing or an appraisal, which follows its own measuring rules.

These are estimates. Confirm the quantities with your supplier before you order.

<h2 id="faq">Frequently asked questions</h2>

### How do I calculate the square footage of a room?

Measure the length and the width in feet and multiply them. A room 12 ft long and 10 ft wide is 12 × 10 = 120 square feet. If a side has inches, turn them into feet first by dividing by 12, or type the measurement as you read it, such as 12 ft 6 in, and the calculator converts it for you.

### How do I convert square feet to square yards?

Divide by 9, because a square yard is 3 ft by 3 ft. A 120 square foot room is 120 ÷ 9 = 13.33 square yards. The calculator shows square yards next to square feet, which helps when a quote for carpet is given by the square yard.

### How do I convert square feet to square meters?

Multiply by 0.09290304, the exact number of square meters in one square foot. A 120 square foot room is about 11.15 square meters. To go the other way, divide square meters by 0.09290304, or press Metric (m, cm) and type the sides in meters.

### How do I enter feet and inches?

Type them as you read the tape: 12 ft 6 in, or 12 ft 6 1/2 in for a fraction. A plain number is read in feet, so type 150 in to mean 150 inches. A room 12 ft 6 in by 10 ft is 125.00 square feet.

### How do I find the square footage of an L-shaped room?

Split it into two rectangles that do not overlap and add their areas. For example, a 12 ft by 10 ft main area with a 6 ft by 4 ft nook is 120 + 24 = 144 square feet. Run the calculator once for each rectangle and add the results.

### How much extra flooring should I buy?

The calculator adds nothing for waste. Installers commonly add about 10 percent for cuts on a simple layout, and more for a diagonal or patterned one. For a 120 square foot room, 10 percent extra is 132 square feet. Check the recommendation of your product and your installer, then round up to whole boxes.

### How do I find the square footage of a wall?

Multiply the width of the wall by its height. A wall 12 ft wide and 8 ft high is 96 square feet. For paint, subtract each door and window as its own rectangle, then compare the result with the coverage printed on the paint label.

### Can I use meters and centimeters?

Yes. Press Metric (m, cm), then type meters or centimeters, such as 3.5 m or 350 cm. A plain number is read in meters, and the result leads with square meters. Switching units never changes your numbers, only how they are written.
