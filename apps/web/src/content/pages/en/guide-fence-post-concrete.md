# How much concrete for a fence post?

A 3 in square post in a hole 9 in wide and 30 in deep takes about two 50 lb bags of fast-setting concrete mix, according to Quikrete's data sheet. The concrete is the hole minus the post, so size the hole first: its width and its depth decide how many bags each post takes, and the number of posts decides how many bags the fence takes.

## Size the hole

Quikrete's instructions for setting posts give two rules:

- **Width:** dig the hole about 3 times the width of the post.
- **Depth:** dig it 1/3 of the post's overall length. A post 8 ft long goes about 32 in into the ground and stands about 5 ft 4 in above it.

Your local code, the soil and the frost depth can call for a deeper hole, so check before you dig.

## Work out the concrete

Quikrete's data sheet for its Fast-Setting Concrete Mix gives a worked case: two 50 lb bags set a 3 in square post in a hole 9 in wide and 30 in deep. Here is the arithmetic behind it:

- The hole is a cylinder: π × 4.5 in × 4.5 in × 30 in = 1,909 cubic inches, or 1.10 cubic feet.
- The post takes 3 in × 3 in × 30 in = 270 cubic inches, or 0.16 cubic feet, so the space around it is 0.95 cubic feet.
- The top 4 in of the hole get soil instead of concrete, so the concrete is about 26 in deep: 0.82 cubic feet.
- A 50 lb bag makes about 0.375 cubic feet: 0.82 ÷ 0.375 = 2.2 bags. A 60 lb bag makes about 0.45 cubic feet: 1.8 bags.

The data sheet rounds to two bags of 50 lb. A hole dug a little wide takes more, so a spare bag on site is cheaper than a second trip. For a fence of 10 such posts, the concrete is about 8.2 cubic feet: 22 bags of 50 lb or 19 bags of 60 lb.

## Set the post

The same instructions, step by step:

1. Stand the post in the hole and hold it straight.
2. Pour the dry mix until it is 3 to 4 in from the top of the hole.
3. Add about 1 gallon of water for each 50 lb bag, until the powder is soaked. In a hole deeper than 30 in, fill to 30 in or less first, let the water soak in, then add the rest.
4. Fill the top 3 to 4 in with sod or the soil you dug out.

Fast-Setting Concrete Mix sets in 20 to 40 minutes. Wait 4 hours before you hang rails or a gate on the post.

## Use the calculator for the whole fence

In the concrete calculator, pick the column, type the diameter and the depth of one hole, and set the number of pieces to the number of posts. It works out the volume of every hole with the extra you choose, so it errs on the safe side: it does not take out the post or the soil on top. Its bag counts are for regular concrete mix of 40, 60 or 80 lb; for a fast-setting mix, divide the volume by the yield printed on its bag.
