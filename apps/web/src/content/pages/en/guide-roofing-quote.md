# How to quote a roofing job

A roofing quote has three parts: the shingles and the underlayment, everything the roof needs around them, and the work. The first comes from the size and the pitch of the roof; the other two you list so nothing is left out of the price.

## Measure the roof in squares

Roofers order and price by the square, 100 square feet of roof. A roof is bigger than the footprint of the house, because it slopes: multiply the footprint by the slope factor of the pitch. A footprint 40 ft by 30 ft at 6 in 12 has 1,341.64 square feet of roof, 13.42 squares. The guide [How to measure a roof and understand its pitch](/guides/how-to-measure-roof-pitch) shows how to read the pitch on site, and the [Roof pitch chart: pitch to degrees, percent and rafter factor](/roofing/roof-pitch-chart) gives the slope factor of every pitch from 1/12 to 24/12.

## Count the shingles and the underlayment

- **Shingles.** Add the extra first: about 10 percent for a simple gable roof, for the starter course, the caps and the cuts, and 15 percent or more for a hip roof or a roof with many valleys. Architectural shingles come about three bundles to a square, 33.33 square feet a bundle. The example roof takes 45 bundles at 10 percent; as a hip roof at 15 percent, 1,341.64 × 1.15 ÷ 33.33 = 46.3, so 47 bundles.
- **Underlayment.** A 48 in by 250 ft roll of synthetic underlayment covers about 957 square feet in one layer, with the laps the code asks for. From 4 in 12 up, one layer: the example roof takes 2 rolls. From 2 in 12 to 4 in 12, the code asks for two layers, so about twice as many. Below 2 in 12, asphalt shingles are not allowed: choose a covering made for low slopes.

## List everything around the shingles

The roofing calculator does not count these, so each is a line of the quote, priced from your suppliers:

- **Tear-off and disposal:** removing the old roof, the dumpster and the haul-off.
- **Deck repairs:** rotten boards only show once the old roof is off. Put a price per sheet in the quote instead of a guess.
- **Drip edge** along the eaves and the rakes.
- **Starter strips** along the eaves, and **hip and ridge caps**.
- **Flashing** at walls, chimneys, skylights and vents.
- **Ice barrier**, where ice dams form: the code asks for it from the eaves to 24 inches inside the wall line.
- **Nails**, as the shingle maker's instructions ask.
- **Permit and inspection**, where your local code requires them.

## List the work

Break the labor into the steps you will do, so you can price each and the client can see what is included: tear-off, deck repairs, underlayment, drip edge, starter, shingles, flashing, caps, and cleaning up the nails and the debris. Price them the way you work, usually by the square, and keep the same method from job to job so you can compare.

A steep roof or a roof with many hips, valleys and dormers takes more time per square than a simple gable roof. Say in the quote how you priced it.

## Write it down

Put in the quote what the client can check: the squares, the shingle product and color, the layers of underlayment, whether the old roof comes off and how many layers it has, the price per sheet of deck repair, and what is not included, such as gutters or siding. Say how long the price holds, because material prices change.

If your state asks for a license for the job, put its number on the quote; the guide [How to get a contractor license in the US](/guides/how-to-get-a-contractor-license) shows where to check.
