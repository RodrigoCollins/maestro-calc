# How much does a ton of gravel cover?

One ton of gravel covers about 88 square feet at 2 in deep, 59 at 3 in and 44 at 4 in, once it is compacted. Spread loose and left that way, the same ton covers about 126, 84 and 63 square feet. Start with the depth, then count the tons.

## What one ton fills

Compacted gravel weighs approximately 1.83 tons per cubic yard, and loose gravel approximately 1.29, the figures the gravel calculator uses. So one ton fills 1 ÷ 1.83 = 0.55 cubic yards once compacted, or 14.75 cubic feet, and 1 ÷ 1.29 = 0.78 cubic yards loose, or 20.93 cubic feet. Loose gravel holds more air between the stones, so the same ton takes more room until it is packed down.

## Square feet per ton

Divide the cubic feet of one ton by the depth in feet. 2 in is 1/6 ft, 3 in is 1/4 ft and 4 in is 1/3 ft:

| Depth | Compacted   | Loose        |
| ----- | ----------- | ------------ |
| 2 in  | 88.52 sq ft | 125.58 sq ft |
| 3 in  | 59.02 sq ft | 83.72 sq ft  |
| 4 in  | 44.26 sq ft | 62.79 sq ft  |

Use the compacted column for a driveway, a base or a path you tamp down, and the loose column for a layer you spread and leave. These are tons with nothing lost: the ground is never perfectly flat, and some gravel works into the soil, so plan on some extra.

## Tons for an area

An area 10 ft by 10 ft, compacted, with no extra, takes:

- at 2 in deep: 1.13 tons;
- at 3 in deep: 1.69 tons;
- at 4 in deep: 2.26 tons.

The same area 3 in deep, spread loose, takes 1.19 tons. The [gravel calculator](/gravel-calculator) gives those figures for each depth, and rounds them only when it shows them.

For a path 50 ft long and 3 ft wide, 3 in deep and spread loose, with the calculator's 5 percent extra, the layer is 1.46 cubic yards: 1.88 tons.

## Tons for a driveway

A driveway 20 ft by 10 ft with a top layer 4 in deep, compacted, is 2.47 cubic yards in place. With the 5 percent extra the gravel calculator adds by default, it takes 4.74 tons, or 3.68 cubic yards loose if the yard sells by volume.

## Order by the ton or by the yard

Order by weight when the yard sells by the ton. If it sells by the cubic yard, order the loose volume, not the volume in place: the calculator shows it next to the tons. The guide [How to calculate cubic yards](/guides/how-to-calculate-cubic-yards) explains the volume math, inches to feet included. Then round your order up, not down: a little left over costs less than a second delivery. What a cubic yard of gravel, sand, topsoil, concrete or mulch weighs is in [How much does a cubic yard weigh? Materials chart](/landscaping/cubic-yard-weight-chart).

## Sources

- Weight of a cubic yard of gravel, compacted and loose: WisDOT WHRP G22-05, Table E2, `https://wisconsindot.gov/documents2/research/0092-22-05-final-report.pdf`, retrieved 2026-10-28.
- The square feet per ton are arithmetic: the cubic feet of one ton divided by the depth in feet. The 5 percent extra is the gravel calculator's default, and every figure of the areas, the path and the driveway is worked out by the gravel calculator. Gravel weighs differently by stone and by size, so ask your yard what its gravel weighs.
