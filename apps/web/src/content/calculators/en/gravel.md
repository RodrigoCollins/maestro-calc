<h2 id="how-to-use">How to use the gravel calculator</h2>

The calculator tells you how many tons of gravel to order for a rectangle at a given depth, and how many cubic yards that is for a yard that sells by volume. The result updates as you type, so there is nothing to submit.

1. Measure the length and the width of the area, and the depth of gravel you want. Type them the way you read a tape: 20 ft, 12 ft 6 in or 5 ft 3 1/2 in. A plain number is read in feet for the length and the width, and in inches for the depth.
2. Choose how the gravel will be placed. Compacted is for driveways, parking pads and the base under pavers or a shed, where the gravel is rolled or tamped. Loose is for landscaping beds, paths and drainage, where it is spread and left as it falls.
3. Keep the 5 percent extra, or change it for uneven ground, a soft subgrade or material lost when the truck dumps.
4. To see the cost, type the price per ton your yard charges.

The result leads with the tons to order. It also shows the same weight in metric tonnes, the loose volume to order in cubic yards and cubic meters, and the volume the gravel fills in place.

To work in metric, press Metric (m, cm). Lengths are then read in meters and the depth in centimeters, and the result leads with metric tonnes. Switching units never changes your numbers, only how they are written. Use Copy link to send your exact numbers to a partner, a client or the yard, and the Español link to open the same numbers in Spanish.

<h2 id="formulas">Formulas and worked example</h2>

The calculator works from the space the gravel fills to the weight you buy:

- Volume in place = length × width × depth.
- Total volume = volume in place × (1 + extra ÷ 100).
- Weight = total volume × the weight of a cubic yard of gravel placed that way.
- Volume to order = weight ÷ the weight of a cubic yard of loose gravel.

It uses approximately 1.83 tons per cubic yard for compacted gravel and approximately 1.29 tons per cubic yard for loose gravel. Both come from a 2023 Wisconsin DOT study of dense-graded ¾-inch base (WisDOT WHRP G22-05, Table E2). Compacted gravel is denser, so the same space takes more tons. A ton here is a short ton of 2,000 lb; a metric tonne is exactly 0.90718474 short tons (NIST SP 811).

Worked example: a driveway 20 ft long, 10 ft wide and 4 in deep, compacted, with 5 percent extra.

- 4 in is 1/3 ft, so the volume in place is 20 × 10 × 1/3 = 66.67 cubic feet, or 2.47 cubic yards (÷ 27).
- With 5 percent extra, that is 70.00 cubic feet, or 2.59 cubic yards.
- At 1.83 tons per cubic yard: 2.59 × 1.83 = 4.74 tons, which is 4.30 metric tonnes.
- The yard loads it loose, at 1.29 tons per cubic yard: 4.74 ÷ 1.29 = 3.68 cubic yards.
- At $45 a ton, the gravel costs $213.50.

The calculator keeps exact fractions from the first input to the last step and rounds only when it shows a figure. The page [How we calculate](/methodology#gravel) lists the formulas and the cases we test.

<h2 id="tips">Tips and common mistakes</h2>

Order by weight when you can. A cubic yard of gravel weighs a different amount loose and compacted, and a ton is a ton. If the yard sells by the cubic yard, order the loose volume the calculator shows, not the volume in place.

Gravel compacts. If you order the compacted volume as loose yards, you come up short by about a third. That is why the calculator turns the weight back into loose cubic yards for the order.

A gravel driveway is usually built in layers: a coarse base and a finer top course. Run the calculator once per layer, with the depth of that layer, and add the tons.

Measure the depth after the ground is graded. A low spot of 2 inches across a large area adds up quickly, so keep some extra for an uneven subgrade.

Wet gravel weighs more. If the pile was rained on, the same tons cover a little less ground.

The weight of a cubic yard changes with the stone, its size and how much fine material it carries. Confirm the weight per yard with your supplier before you order a large load.

These are estimates. Confirm the quantities with your supplier before you order.

<h2 id="faq">Frequently asked questions</h2>

### How much does a yard of gravel weigh?

About 1.3 tons loose and about 1.8 tons compacted, depending on the stone. The calculator uses approximately 1.29 tons per cubic yard for loose gravel and 1.83 for compacted gravel, from a 2023 Wisconsin DOT study of dense-graded base.

### How many tons of gravel do I need for a driveway?

Multiply length × width × depth to get the volume, add the extra, then multiply the cubic yards by about 1.83 for a compacted driveway. A driveway 20 ft by 10 ft and 4 in deep needs about 4.74 tons with 5 percent extra.

### How deep should driveway gravel be?

Many driveways use 4 to 8 inches in total, in two or three layers. Heavy trucks and soft soil need more depth. Follow your local practice and the advice of your supplier, and run the calculator once per layer.

### Should I choose compacted or loose?

Choose compacted when the gravel will be rolled or tamped, such as a driveway, a parking pad or a base for pavers. Choose loose when it is spread and left, such as a path, a bed or a drainage trench. The same space takes more tons when it is compacted.

### How do I convert cubic yards of gravel to tons?

Multiply the cubic yards by the weight of a cubic yard placed that way: about 1.83 tons compacted or 1.29 tons loose. For example, 2.59 cubic yards compacted is about 4.74 tons.

### Why does the volume to order differ from the volume in place?

The yard measures gravel loose in the truck, and loose gravel takes more space than compacted gravel. The calculator turns the weight you need back into loose cubic yards, so 2.59 cubic yards compacted becomes about 3.68 cubic yards to order.

### How much extra gravel should I add?

The calculator starts at 5 percent, which covers small spills and minor dips. Add more for uneven ground, a soft subgrade or a long carry. Removing the extra from the example drops the order from 4.74 to 4.52 tons.

### Can I use meters and centimeters?

Yes. Press Metric (m, cm), then type the length and the width in meters and the depth in centimeters. The result leads with metric tonnes. Switching units never changes your numbers, only how they are written.
