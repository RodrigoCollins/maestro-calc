<h2 id="how-to-use">How to use the roofing calculator</h2>

The calculator turns the footprint of a roof and its pitch into the area of the roof in squares, the bundles of asphalt shingles and the rolls of underlayment to buy. The result updates as you type, so there is nothing to submit.

1. Measure the footprint of the roof from above: the length and the width of the house, plus the overhangs on each side. Type them the way you read a tape: 40 ft, 40 ft 6 in or 30 ft 1 1/2 in. A plain number is read in feet.
2. Type the pitch as the rise in 12: 6 means the roof rises 6 inches for every 12 inches of run. If you do not know it, the [roof pitch calculator](/roof-pitch-calculator) finds it from a rise and a run.
3. Keep the 10 percent extra for the starter course, the ridge, the hips and the cuts, or change it.
4. To see the costs, type the price per bundle and the price per roll.

The result leads with the area of the roof in squares. A square is 100 square feet, the unit shingles are sold and quoted by. It also shows the same area in square feet and in square meters, the plan of the footprint with its pitch, and the shopping list: the bundles of shingles and the rolls of underlayment.

To work in metric, press Metric (m, cm). Lengths are then read in meters, and the result leads with square meters; the pitch is still a rise in 12. Switching units never changes your numbers, only how they are written. Use Copy link to send your exact numbers to a partner, a client or the supplier, and the Español link to open the same numbers in Spanish.

<h2 id="formulas">Formulas and worked example</h2>

The calculator works from the footprint to what you buy:

- Slope factor = √(1 + (pitch ÷ 12)²). A roof is longer than its footprint by this factor on every plane that shares the pitch, whether it is a gable, a hip or a shed roof.
- Roof area = length × width × slope factor; squares = roof area ÷ 100.
- Bundles = roof area × (1 + extra ÷ 100) ÷ 33⅓ square feet, rounded up: three bundles cover a square.
- One roll of underlayment is 48 in by 250 ft. From a pitch of 4 in 12 the code asks for one layer lapped 2 inches, with 4-inch end laps, so a roll covers (250 ft − 4 in) × (48 − 2) in = 957.06 square feet. From 2 in 12 up to 4 in 12 it asks for two layers, so a roll covers half of that.
- Rolls = roof area × (1 + extra ÷ 100) ÷ what a roll covers, rounded up.

Worked example: a house 40 ft by 30 ft with its overhangs, a pitch of 6 in 12 and 10 percent extra.

- The footprint is 40 × 30 = 1,200 square feet. At 6 in 12 the slope factor is √(1 + 0.25) = 1.118.
- The roof is 1,200 × 1.118 = 1,341.64 square feet: 13.42 squares, or 124.64 square meters.
- With 10 percent extra that is 1,475.80 square feet, and 1,475.80 ÷ 33.33 = 44.3, so you buy 45 bundles.
- At 6 in 12 the underlayment is one layer: 1,475.80 ÷ 957.06 = 1.5, so 2 rolls.
- At $35 a bundle the shingles cost $1,575.00, and at $90 a roll the underlayment costs $180.00.

The calculator keeps exact fractions from the first input to the last step; the square root is kept to twenty decimals. The purchase counts are rounded up, and every other figure is rounded only when it is shown. The page [How we calculate](/methodology#roofing) lists the formulas and the cases we test.

<h2 id="tips">Tips and common mistakes</h2>

Measure the footprint with the overhangs. The eaves and the rakes are roof too: a 1 ft overhang on every side of a 38 ft by 28 ft house makes a 40 ft by 30 ft footprint.

Do not measure the roof surface and multiply by the slope factor again. Either measure the footprint on the ground and let the calculator apply the pitch, or measure each plane on the roof and add them up yourself.

Keep the extra for the starter course along the eaves, the hip and ridge caps, and the cuts at valleys. A roof with many hips and valleys takes more: 15 percent is common for a hip roof.

Mind the pitch. Asphalt shingles need at least 2 in 12, and from 2 in 12 up to 4 in 12 the code asks for two layers of underlayment; the calculator doubles the rolls there and says so in the answer. Below 2 in 12, choose a covering made for low slopes.

The calculator does not count nails, drip edge, starter strips, ridge caps, flashing or an ice barrier. Where ice dams form, the code asks for an ice barrier from the eaves to 24 inches inside the wall line; check your local code and ask your supplier.

These are estimates. Confirm the quantities with your supplier before you order.

<h2 id="faq">Frequently asked questions</h2>

### How many bundles of shingles do I need?

Find the area of the roof in squares, add about 10 percent for waste and multiply by three: most architectural shingles come three bundles to a square. A 40 ft by 30 ft footprint at 6 in 12 has 13.42 squares of roof and needs 45 bundles.

### What is a square in roofing?

A square is 100 square feet of roof. Shingles, underlayment and labor are priced by the square, and a bundle of architectural shingles covers about a third of one.

### How do I find the area of my roof?

Multiply the length and the width of the footprint, overhangs included, by the slope factor of the pitch. At 6 in 12 the factor is 1.118, so a 1,200 square foot footprint has 1,341.64 square feet of roof.

### How much underlayment do I need?

A 48 in by 250 ft roll of synthetic underlayment covers 1,000 square feet before laps, about 957 square feet laid in one layer with the laps the code asks for. The example roof needs 2 rolls. Below 4 in 12 the code asks for two layers, so you need about twice as many.

### Does the pitch change the number of shingles?

Yes. A steeper roof has more surface over the same footprint. The same 40 ft by 30 ft house needs 45 bundles at 6 in 12 and 57 bundles at 12 in 12.

### What is the minimum pitch for asphalt shingles?

The International Residential Code allows asphalt shingles from a pitch of 2 in 12, with two layers of underlayment up to 4 in 12. Below 2 in 12 the calculator warns you and you need another covering.

### How much extra should I add?

The calculator starts at 10 percent, enough for a simple gable roof. Use 15 percent or more for a hip roof or a roof with many valleys, dormers and penetrations.

### Can I use meters?

Yes. Press Metric (m, cm), then type the length and the width in meters. The result leads with square meters, and the pitch is still a rise in 12. Switching units never changes your numbers, only how they are written.
