# How to read a tape measure and add fractions

A tape measure in the United States reads in feet, inches and fractions of an inch. Reading it is a matter of knowing what each mark is worth. Adding what you read is a matter of a common denominator and carrying inches into feet.

## The marks of one inch

Most tapes divide each inch into 16 equal parts, so the smallest step is 1/16 inch. The length of a line tells you its fraction:

1. The longest line is the whole inch, with its number.
2. The next longest is the half inch, 1/2.
3. Then the quarters, 1/4 and 3/4.
4. Then the eighths, 1/8, 3/8, 5/8 and 7/8.
5. The shortest lines are the sixteenths, 1/16, 3/16 and so on.

To read a length, take the last whole inch before the end of what you measure and count the sixteenths after it. Eleven sixteenths past 5 is 5 11/16 inches. When the count is even, halve both numbers until one is odd: 6/16 is 3/8, and 8/16 is 1/2.

Many tapes also highlight a mark every 16 inches, the usual spacing of wall studs, and some add a small mark every 19.2 inches, which divides an 8-foot length (96 inches) into five equal spaces.

## Feet and inches

A foot is 12 inches. Past the first foot, some tapes number the inches only and some show feet and inches; either way, 15 inches is 1 ft 3 in. Write a length as feet, then inches, then the fraction: 12 ft 6 in, or 3 ft 8 1/4 in.

## Adding and subtracting

Add the fractions first, over a common denominator, then the inches, then the feet, carrying as you go:

- 5/8 + 3/16 is 10/16 + 3/16 = 13/16.
- 3/4 + 7/16 is 12/16 + 7/16 = 19/16, which is 1 3/16: carry the 1 into the inches.
- 12 ft 6 in + 3 ft 8 1/4 in is 15 ft 14 1/4 in. Fourteen inches is a foot and 2 inches, so the sum is 16 ft 2 1/4 in.

To subtract, borrow when the top is smaller. For 10 ft 2 in − 4 ft 7 1/2 in, borrow a foot: 10 ft 2 in is 9 ft 14 in, and 9 ft 14 in − 4 ft 7 1/2 in = 5 ft 6 1/2 in.

## Dividing a length into equal parts

Turn the length into inches, divide, and turn the result back. 8 ft 5 in is 101 inches; in four equal parts, that is 25 1/4 inches each, or 2 ft 1 1/4 in.

Most divisions do not land on a sixteenth. 5 ft 1 in (61 inches) in three parts is 20.333... inches each, and the nearest sixteenth is 20 5/16 in. Three of those make 60 15/16 inches, 1/16 short of the whole, so mark the parts from the start of the length rather than one after another, and the small error does not add up.

## Decimals and metric

To turn a decimal of an inch into sixteenths, multiply it by 16 and round: 0.3 inch is 4.8 sixteenths, so about 5/16. For metric, an inch is exactly 25.4 millimeters and a foot is exactly 304.8 millimeters: 16 ft 2 1/4 in is 194.25 inches, or approximately 4.934 meters.
