<h2 id="how-to-use">How to use the topsoil calculator</h2>

The calculator tells you how much topsoil an area needs, in cubic yards for a bulk delivery and in whole bags for a trip to the store. Use it for a new lawn, a garden bed, a raised bed or a low spot you want to fill. The result updates as you type, so there is nothing to submit.

1. Measure the length and the width of the area, and decide how deep the new soil will be. Type them the way you read a tape: 10 ft, 12 ft 6 in or 5 ft 3 1/2 in. A plain number is read in feet for the length and the width, and in inches for the depth.
2. Keep the 10 percent extra for settling, or change it. Loose topsoil packs down after it is spread, watered and walked on, so you need more than the space it fills on the first day.
3. Choose the bag size you will buy: 0.75, 1 or 1.5 cubic feet, the sizes printed on common retail bags.
4. To see the cost, type the price per bag, the price per cubic yard delivered in bulk, or both.

The result leads with the cubic yards. It also shows the same volume in cubic feet and cubic meters, the volume before the extra, and the number of bags of the size you chose.

To work in metric, press Metric (m, cm). Lengths are then read in meters and the depth in centimeters, and the result leads with cubic meters. Switching units never changes your numbers, only how they are written. Use Copy link to send your exact numbers to a partner, a client or the supplier, and the Español link to open the same numbers in Spanish.

<h2 id="formulas">Formulas and worked example</h2>

The calculator works from the size of the area to what you buy:

- Volume = length × width × depth.
- Total volume = volume × (1 + extra ÷ 100).
- Cubic yards = total volume in cubic feet ÷ 27.
- Bags = total volume ÷ bag size, rounded up to a whole bag.

No weight or density is involved. A bag holds the volume printed on it, and a cubic yard is exactly 27 cubic feet. A foot is exactly 0.3048 meters (NIST SP 811), which gives the cubic meters.

Worked example: a garden area 10 ft long, 10 ft wide and 4 in deep, with 10 percent extra and 0.75 cu ft bags.

- 4 in is 1/3 ft, so the volume is 10 × 10 × 1/3 = 33.33 cubic feet.
- With 10 percent extra for settling, that is 36.67 cubic feet.
- Divided by 27, that is 1.36 cubic yards, or 1.04 cubic meters.
- 36.67 ÷ 0.75 = 48.9 bags, so you buy 49 bags of 0.75 cu ft. With 1 cu ft bags it is 37 bags, and with 1.5 cu ft bags it is 25.
- At $3 a bag, 49 bags cost $147.00. At $45 a cubic yard in bulk, the same soil costs $61.11 before delivery.

The calculator keeps exact fractions from the first input to the last step. The bag count is rounded up, and every other figure is rounded only when it is shown. The page [How we calculate](/methodology#topsoil) lists the formulas and the cases we test.

<h2 id="tips">Tips and common mistakes</h2>

Compare bags and bulk early. Small bags add up fast: the example area takes 49 bags, which is a lot of lifting and a lot of plastic. Past a cubic yard or so, a bulk load is often cheaper and easier, even with a delivery fee. Type both prices and the calculator shows both costs.

Measure the depth you are adding, not the depth of the soil already there. To raise a low spot by 2 inches, use 2 inches, even if the good soil under it is deeper.

Split an odd-shaped area into rectangles, run the calculator for each one and add the results. For a gentle curve, measure the longest length and the widest width, and the extra covers the difference.

Plan where the delivery goes. A dump truck leaves the pile where it can reach, so keep a clear spot close to the work and cover the pile if rain is coming.

Spread and rake the soil in layers on a deep fill, and water it lightly between layers. It settles more evenly, and you see sooner if you need more.

Check what is in the soil you buy. Screened topsoil, garden soil and fill dirt are sold under different names and at different prices, and they do not do the same job. Ask the supplier which one fits a lawn or a bed.

These are estimates. Confirm the quantities with your supplier before you order.

<h2 id="faq">Frequently asked questions</h2>

### How much topsoil do I need?

Multiply length × width × depth, with the depth in feet, then add the extra for settling. An area 10 ft by 10 ft and 4 in deep holds 33.33 cubic feet. With 10 percent extra that is 36.67 cubic feet, or 1.36 cubic yards: 49 bags of 0.75 cu ft.

### How many bags of topsoil are in a yard?

A cubic yard is exactly 27 cubic feet, so it takes 36 bags of 0.75 cu ft, 27 bags of 1 cu ft or 18 bags of 1.5 cu ft. Round up to whole bags when you buy.

### How deep should topsoil be?

It depends on the job. A new lawn often gets 4 to 6 inches, a vegetable or flower bed often more, and an existing lawn only a thin top dressing. Follow your local practice and the advice of your supplier, then type the depth you choose.

### How much area does a yard of topsoil cover?

One cubic yard covers about 324 square feet at 1 inch deep, 162 square feet at 2 inches, 81 square feet at 4 inches and 54 square feet at 6 inches, before any extra.

### Why does the calculator add 10 percent?

Loose topsoil settles after it is spread, watered and walked on, so the space it fills shrinks. The calculator starts at 10 percent for that. Removing the extra from the example drops the order from 1.36 to 1.23 cubic yards, or from 49 bags to 45.

### Is topsoil cheaper in bags or in bulk?

It depends on the size of the job and the delivery fee. Type the price per bag and the price per cubic yard, and the calculator shows both costs. For the example area, 49 bags at $3 cost $147.00, and bulk at $45 a yard costs $61.11 before delivery.

### Which bag size should I choose?

Choose the size your store sells. The volume is printed on the bag, usually 0.75, 1 or 1.5 cubic feet. The volume of soil you need stays the same, and only the number of bags changes.

### Can I use meters and centimeters?

Yes. Press Metric (m, cm), then type the length and the width in meters and the depth in centimeters. The result leads with cubic meters. Switching units never changes your numbers, only how they are written.
