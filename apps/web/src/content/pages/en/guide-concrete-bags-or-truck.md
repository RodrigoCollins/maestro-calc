# Concrete: bags or a truck?

For a small pour, bags are usually simpler. For a big one, a ready-mix truck is. In between, the answer depends on what the plant charges for a small load, so it pays to do the math before you buy.

## Start with the volume

Work out how much concrete the job needs, with some extra for waste and uneven ground. The concrete calculator below does it for slabs, footings, columns and steps, and gives the result in cubic yards and in bags.

## What one cubic yard means in bags

Ready-mix is sold by the cubic yard, and a cubic yard is 27 cubic feet. A bag of concrete mix makes approximately 0.60 cubic feet (80 lb bag), 0.45 cubic feet (60 lb) or 0.30 cubic feet (40 lb), so one cubic yard takes about:

- 45 bags of 80 lb,
- 60 bags of 60 lb, or
- 90 bags of 40 lb.

That is about 3,600 pounds of dry mix to haul, mix and place by hand for every cubic yard.

## What a small truck load costs

A plant prices concrete by the cubic yard, but a truck costs the same to send whether it carries one yard or nine. Below a certain size, most plants add a short-load charge, and many bill a minimum number of yards.

As an example, in a 2020 public bid for ready-mix concrete in Jefferson County, Missouri, all three suppliers added a charge to loads under 5 cubic yards: one charged $35 per yard up to its minimum, and the other two charged a flat fee of $25 to $125 per load, depending on its size, and billed at least 2 yards. Prices and rules change by plant and by year, so ask yours.

## How to decide

1. **Under about 1 cubic yard:** bags usually win. You buy only what you need, there is no minimum, and you can pour at your own pace.
2. **Several cubic yards:** a truck usually wins. The whole pour comes at once from one batch, and nobody mixes tons of concrete by hand.
3. **In between:** compare two prices. Ask the plant for its price per yard, its minimum order, its short-load charge and its delivery fee. Then enter your store's price per bag and the plant's price per yard in the calculator, which totals both for you.

Before you order a truck, check that it can reach the pour or that the chute or a pump can, and have enough help ready to place and finish the concrete as it arrives.
