# How to quote a concrete job

A concrete quote has three parts: the concrete itself, everything the pour needs around it, and the work. The first comes from the size of the pour; the other two you list so nothing is left out of the price.

## Work out the concrete

Measure the pour and turn it into a volume, then add extra for waste and uneven ground. A slab 12 ft by 10 ft and 4 in thick is 12 × 10 × 1/3 = 40 cubic feet; with 10 percent extra it is 44 cubic feet, or about 1.63 cubic yards. In bags, at approximately 0.60 cubic feet per 80 lb bag, that is 74 bags.

Write the thickness in the quote next to the area. A slab 4 in thick and one 6 in thick look the same on a plan and take very different amounts of concrete.

## Decide how it arrives

Below about 1 cubic yard, bags are usually simpler; for several cubic yards, a ready-mix truck usually is. Many plants add a short-load charge or a minimum order below a certain size, so a pour like the 1.63 cubic yards above is worth pricing both ways. The guide [Concrete: bags or a truck?](/guides/concrete-bags-or-truck) walks through the comparison.

## List everything around the concrete

Each of these is a line of the quote, priced from your suppliers:

- **Forms:** boards, stakes and screws or nails for the edges.
- **Base:** the gravel under the slab, with its depth. The [gravel calculator](/gravel-calculator) works out the tons.
- **Reinforcement:** rebar, wire mesh or fibers, as the design and the local code ask.
- **Delivery:** the plant's delivery fee and any short-load charge, or the trips to bring the bags.
- **Rentals:** a mixer, a pump or finishing tools, if the job needs them.
- **Permit and inspection:** where your local code requires them.
- **Haul-off:** removing old concrete, soil from the excavation and form scraps.

## List the work

Break the labor into the steps you will do, so you can price each and the client can see what is included: digging and grading, setting forms, placing and compacting the base, setting reinforcement, placing and finishing the concrete, curing, stripping forms and cleaning up. Price them the way you work, by the square foot, by the cubic yard or by the day, and keep the same method from job to job so you can compare.

For a truck pour, have enough help on site to place and finish the concrete as it arrives, and price that crew in.

## Write it down

Put in the quote what the client can check: the area and the thickness, the cubic yards, the base depth, the reinforcement, the finish (broom, smooth or stamped), and what is not included. Say how long the price holds, because material prices change.
