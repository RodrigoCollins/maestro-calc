# How much does a gallon of paint cover?

One gallon of interior latex paint covers about 350 to 400 square feet in one coat, according to the manufacturer's data sheet. Two coats take twice the paint, so the same gallon covers about 175 square feet of wall.

## Square feet per gallon

Divide the coverage of one coat by the coats. Using the low end, 350 square feet, keeps the estimate from coming up short:

| Coats | Square feet per gallon |
| ----- | ---------------------- |
| 1     | 350 sq ft              |
| 2     | 175 sq ft              |
| 3     | 116.67 sq ft           |

Check the label of the paint you buy, because the coverage changes with the product, the color and the surface.

## What changes the coverage

- **The surface.** Bare drywall, stucco and block soak up more paint than a wall that was painted before. Plan on a coat of primer there, so the paint covers evenly.
- **The color.** A new color usually takes two coats, so count the coats before you count the gallons.
- **The texture.** A rough or textured wall has more surface than its length times its height, so the same gallon covers less of it.

## Gallons for a room

A bedroom 12 ft by 12 ft with 8 ft walls has 2 × (12 + 12) × 8 = 384.00 square feet of walls. Leaving out about 20 square feet for its door and about 15 for each of two windows, 334.00 square feet remain to paint.

At 350 square feet a gallon, the [paint calculator](/paint-calculator) gives:

- one coat: 0.95 gallons, 1 gallon can;
- two coats: 668 ÷ 350 = 1.91 gallons (7.22 liters), 2 gallon cans;
- three coats: 2.86 gallons, 3 gallon cans.

With the same 8 ft walls, one door and two windows, a 10 ft by 10 ft room has 270.00 square feet to paint: 1.54 gallons for two coats, 2 cans. A 12 ft by 16 ft room has 398.00 square feet: 2.27 gallons, 3 cans. The calculator rounds up to whole gallon cans only once, at the end, so the paint never comes up short.

## Buy it once

Buy the paint for the whole job at once when you can. Cans mixed at different times can differ a little in color. Measure the ceiling and the trim apart: they often take a different product.
