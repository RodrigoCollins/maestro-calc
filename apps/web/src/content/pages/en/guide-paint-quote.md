# How to quote a painting job

A painting quote has three parts: the paint, the supplies and the prep, and the work. The paint comes from the size of the walls; the prep is where two quotes for the same room differ most, so look at the walls before you price anything.

## Measure the walls

Measure the length and the width of the room at the floor and the height of the walls. The walls are 2 × (length + width) × height; take out about 20 square feet for each door and 15 for each window.

A bedroom 12 ft by 12 ft with 8 ft walls, one door and two windows has 2 × (12 + 12) × 8 = 384 square feet of walls, and 384 − 20 − 15 − 15 = 334 square feet to paint.

Measure the ceiling, the trim and the doors apart. They often take a different product, so they are lines of their own.

## Work out the paint

Multiply the area by the coats and divide by the coverage of one gallon. A Sherwin-Williams data sheet gives 350 to 400 square feet per gallon for one coat of an interior latex paint; using the low end, 350, keeps the estimate from coming up short. Two coats on the bedroom are 668 square feet, and 668 ÷ 350 = 1.91 gallons: 2 gallon cans. The [paint calculator](/paint-calculator) does this math for any room.

Check the coverage on the label of the paint you use, because it changes with the product, the color and the surface. Bare drywall, stucco and block soak up more paint; plan on a coat of primer there, and price it as its own line.

Buy the paint for the whole job at once when you can. Cans mixed at different times can differ a little in color.

## List the supplies and the prep

Each of these is a line of the quote, priced from your suppliers:

- **Primer,** where the surface needs it.
- **Patching:** compound and sandpaper for holes and cracks.
- **Protection:** painter's tape, plastic and drop cloths for floors and furniture.
- **Tools:** roller covers, brushes and trays that wear out on the job.
- **Caulk** for the gaps between the trim and the wall.
- **Rentals:** ladders or scaffolding, if the job needs them.

## List the work

Break the labor into the steps you will do, so you can price each and the client can see what is included: moving and covering the furniture, cleaning the walls, patching and sanding, caulking, priming, cutting in, rolling each coat, and cleaning up with the touch-ups. Price them the way you work, by the square foot, by the room or by the day, and keep the same method from job to job so you can compare.

## Write it down

Put in the quote what the client can check: the rooms and surfaces, the product, the color and the sheen (flat, eggshell, satin or semi-gloss), the number of coats, the prep that is included and what is not, such as the ceilings, the trim or moving the furniture. Say how long the price holds, because material prices change.

If your state asks for a license for the job, put its number on the quote; the guide [How to get a contractor license in the US](/guides/how-to-get-a-contractor-license) shows where to check.
