# About Maestro Calc

Maestro Calc builds jobsite calculators that estimate what to buy. We write in English and Spanish for builders and homeowners in the US.

Everything on this site comes from the Maestro Calc Editorial Team. We do not publish under personal names, and we do not claim credentials we do not hold.

<h2 id="editorial-policy">Editorial policy</h2>

Each policy and methodology page says when it was last updated. We present estimates as estimates. The [methodology page](/methodology) explains how each number is worked out, and our [editorial policy](/editorial-policy) says where the numbers come from, how we check them and when we review them. The site has no advertising or sponsors today, so nothing here is shaped by one.

<!-- Raw h2 elements keep each section anchor stable if a heading is reworded. -->

Every calculator goes through the same steps before it is published:

1. Its formulas are written out on the [methodology page](/methodology), with the source of every yield, coverage or code value: manufacturer data sheets (QUIKRETE, USG, Sherwin-Williams and GAF), the 2021 International Residential Code, a Wisconsin Department of Transportation research report for the weight of gravel, and NIST Special Publication 811 for unit conversions.
2. The arithmetic uses exact fractions from the first input to the last step, and rounds only what it shows you.
3. Each calculator is tested against cases worked out by hand, and the methodology page publishes those cases so you can check them yourself.
4. Each page of a common job, such as a 10 × 10 ft slab or a 6/12 roof pitch, is worked out by the same calculator when the site is built, and nearly every one shows its arithmetic step by step.

<h2 id="ai-use">How we use AI</h2>

Our content is AI-assisted and verified by people. We use AI tools to help write text and code. People review the text, every formula is tested against cases checked by hand, and each calculator page cites its sources.
