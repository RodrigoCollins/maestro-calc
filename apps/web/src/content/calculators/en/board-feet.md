<h2 id="how-to-use">How to use the board feet calculator</h2>

A board foot is the unit lumber is sold by: a piece 1 inch thick, 12 inches wide and 1 foot long. The calculator gives the board feet of any piece of lumber from its thickness, its width and its length, multiplies it by the number of pieces, and prices the lot when you know the price per board foot. The result updates as you type.

1. Enter the thickness and the width in inches. For framing lumber, type the size in its name: a 2x4 is 2 in by 4 in. A plain number is read in inches, and fractions work too, such as 1 1/2.
2. Enter the length in feet, the way the yard sells it: 8 ft, 10 ft, 12 ft or 16 ft. A plain number is read in feet; 30 in also works.
3. Enter the number of pieces, and the price per board foot if you have a quote. The cost appears once a price is typed.
4. Read the board feet. The large figure is the total for every piece; the panel also shows the board feet of one piece, the volume in cubic feet and in cubic meters, and the cost.

To work in metric, press Metric (m, cm). The thickness and the width are then read in centimeters and the length in meters, and the result is still given in board feet, the unit lumber prices are quoted in. Use Copy link to send the exact tally to a supplier or a client, and the Español link to open the same numbers in Spanish.

<h2 id="formulas">Formulas and worked example</h2>

The board measure of a piece is its thickness in inches, times its width in inches, times its length in feet, divided by 12:

- Board feet of one piece = thickness (in) × width (in) × length (ft) ÷ 12.
- Board feet of the lot = board feet of one piece × the number of pieces.
- Cubic feet = board feet ÷ 12, because a board foot is 1 in × 12 in × 12 in, which is exactly 144 cubic inches, a twelfth of a cubic foot.
- Cost = board feet × price per board foot.

The American Softwood Lumber Standard (DOC PS 20-20, section 2.2) defines the board measure as the nominal thickness in inches, times the nominal width in feet, times the length in feet. The width in feet is the width in inches divided by 12, which is where the ÷ 12 of the formula comes from.

Worked example: ten 2x4 studs 8 ft long, at $1.50 a board foot.

- One piece is 2 in × 4 in × 8 ft ÷ 12 = 64 ÷ 12 = 5.33 board feet.
- Ten pieces are 10 × 64 ÷ 12 = 640 ÷ 12 = 53.33 board feet.
- In cubic feet, 53.33 ÷ 12 = 4.44 cubic feet, which is 0.126 cubic meters.
- At $1.50 a board foot, the lot costs 640 ÷ 12 × 1.50 = $80.00.

The calculator keeps exact fractions until it shows the figures, so the cost is worked from 640/12 board feet, not from the rounded 53.33. The page [How we calculate](/methodology#board-feet) lists the formulas and the cases we test.

<h2 id="tips">Nominal and actual sizes, and common mistakes</h2>

A 2x4 is not 2 inches by 4 inches. The name is its nominal size; once the piece is dried and planed, PS 20-20, Table 3, sets its minimum dressed size at 1 1/2 in by 3 1/2 in. The same table gives a dry 2x6 as 1 1/2 by 5 1/2 in, a 2x8 as 1 1/2 by 7 1/4 in, a 2x10 as 1 1/2 by 9 1/4 in, a 2x12 as 1 1/2 by 11 1/4 in, a 4x4 as 3 1/2 by 3 1/2 in, and a 1x6 board as 3/4 by 5 1/2 in.

Board feet of softwood lumber are counted on the nominal size: the standard's rule takes the nominal thickness and the nominal width. Type 2 and 4 for a 2x4 to check a yard's tally or an invoice. Type the measured size when you need the wood actually in the piece, for a weight or for a comparison between suppliers: a 2x4x8 is 5.33 board feet by its name, and 3.50 board feet of wood at its dressed size.

The length is not reduced: an 8 ft stud is sold and counted as 8 ft. Lumber comes in even lengths of feet, so a cut list of 7 ft pieces is still bought, and counted, in 8 ft pieces.

Hardwood is often sold rough, with the thickness written in quarters of an inch: 4/4 is 1 in, 5/4 is 1 1/4 in and 8/4 is 2 in. Ask the dealer whether a tally uses the rough or the surfaced size, and type the size the price is quoted on.

The most common mistake is mixing units. The thickness and the width are in inches and the length in feet: a 2x6 10 ft long is 2 × 6 × 10 ÷ 12 = 10 board feet, while 2 × 6 × 120 ÷ 12 would be twelve times too much. If your length is in inches, type it with its unit, such as 120 in, and the calculator converts it.

Board feet measure volume, not cost alone. Prices per board foot change with the species, the grade and the moisture content, so compare quotes on the same species and grade, and confirm the tally with your supplier before you order.

<h2 id="faq">Frequently asked questions</h2>

### How do I calculate board feet?

Multiply the thickness in inches by the width in inches and by the length in feet, then divide by 12. A 2x6 that is 10 ft long is 2 × 6 × 10 ÷ 12 = 10 board feet. For several pieces of the same size, multiply by the number of pieces.

### How many board feet are in a 2x4x8?

5.33 board feet by its nominal size: 2 × 4 × 8 ÷ 12 = 64 ÷ 12. At its dry dressed size, 1 1/2 by 3 1/2 in, the wood in the piece is 3.50 board feet. Lumberyards tally softwood dimension lumber by the nominal size.

### Is a board foot the same as a square foot?

No. A square foot is an area; a board foot is a volume, a square foot of surface 1 inch thick. A 1 in board covers one square foot per board foot, a 2 in plank covers half a square foot per board foot.

### How many board feet are in a cubic foot?

Twelve. A cubic foot is 12 in × 12 in × 12 in, and a board foot is 12 in × 12 in × 1 in, so twelve board feet stack into one cubic foot. A cubic meter holds about 423.78 board feet.

### Should I use the nominal or the actual size?

Use the nominal size to check a softwood lumber tally or a price per board foot, because the American Softwood Lumber Standard counts board measure on nominal sizes. Use the measured size when you need the actual volume of wood, such as for a weight estimate.

### How do I price lumber sold by the board foot?

Find the board feet of the whole order and multiply by the price per board foot. Ten 2x4x8 at $1.50 a board foot are 53.33 board feet and cost $80.00. Type the price in the calculator and it does both steps.

### How many board feet are in a 4x4x8?

10.67 board feet: 4 × 4 × 8 ÷ 12 = 128 ÷ 12. A 4x4 has twice the board feet of a 2x4 of the same length.

### Can I use centimeters and meters?

Yes. Press Metric (m, cm), then type the thickness and the width in centimeters and the length in meters. The result stays in board feet, with the volume in cubic meters beside it.
