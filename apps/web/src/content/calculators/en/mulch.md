<h2 id="how-to-use">How to use the mulch calculator</h2>

The calculator tells you how much mulch a bed needs, in cubic yards for a bulk delivery and in whole bags for a trip to the store. The result updates as you type, so there is nothing to submit.

1. Measure the length and the width of the bed, and decide how deep the mulch will be. Type them the way you read a tape: 20 ft, 12 ft 6 in or 5 ft 3 1/2 in. A plain number is read in feet for the length and the width, and in inches for the depth.
2. Keep the 5 percent extra, or change it. Mulch settles after it is spread, and some is always lost to the wheelbarrow and the edges.
3. Choose the bag size you will buy: 2 or 3 cubic feet, the sizes printed on most retail bags.
4. To see the cost, type the price per bag, the price per cubic yard delivered in bulk, or both.

The result leads with the cubic yards. It also shows the same volume in cubic feet and cubic meters, the volume before the extra, and the number of bags of the size you chose.

To work in metric, press Metric (m, cm). Lengths are then read in meters and the depth in centimeters, and the result leads with cubic meters. Switching units never changes your numbers, only how they are written. Use Copy link to send your exact numbers to a partner, a client or the supplier, and the Español link to open the same numbers in Spanish.

<h2 id="formulas">Formulas and worked example</h2>

The calculator works from the size of the bed to what you buy:

- Volume = length × width × depth.
- Total volume = volume × (1 + extra ÷ 100).
- Cubic yards = total volume in cubic feet ÷ 27.
- Bags = total volume ÷ bag size, rounded up to a whole bag.

No weight or density is involved. A bag holds the volume printed on it, and a cubic yard is exactly 27 cubic feet. A foot is exactly 0.3048 meters (NIST SP 811), which gives the cubic meters.

Worked example: a bed along a fence, 20 ft long, 4 ft wide and 3 in deep, with 5 percent extra and 2 cu ft bags.

- 3 in is 1/4 ft, so the volume is 20 × 4 × 1/4 = 20.00 cubic feet.
- With 5 percent extra, that is 21.00 cubic feet.
- Divided by 27, that is 0.78 cubic yards, or 0.59 cubic meters.
- 21.00 ÷ 2 = 10.5 bags, so you buy 11 bags of 2 cu ft. With 3 cu ft bags, 21.00 ÷ 3 = 7 bags.
- At $5 a bag, 7 bags of 3 cu ft cost $35.00. At $36 a cubic yard in bulk, the same mulch costs $28.00 before delivery.

The calculator keeps exact fractions from the first input to the last step. The bag count is rounded up, and every other figure is rounded only when it is shown. The page [How we calculate](/methodology#mulch) lists the formulas and the cases we test.

<h2 id="tips">Tips and common mistakes</h2>

Measure the depth you want to add, not the depth the bed will have. If there are already 2 inches of old mulch and you want 3, add 1 inch, or rake the old layer out first.

Keep mulch a few inches away from tree trunks and plant stems. A mound of mulch against the bark holds moisture where the tree does not want it, and it wastes material.

Split an odd-shaped bed into rectangles, run the calculator for each one and add the results. For a curved edge, measure the longest length and the widest width, and the extra covers the difference.

Compare bags and bulk before you order. A small bed is usually cheaper in bags, with no delivery fee. Past a cubic yard or two, a bulk load often costs less per yard, even with the delivery. Type both prices and the calculator shows both costs.

Order a little more than you think when the bed is new. Fresh mulch on loose soil settles more than mulch spread over an older layer.

Shredded and chipped mulch settle at different rates, and bark nuggets cover less ground than the same volume of fine mulch. If your supplier gives a coverage for their product, trust it over a general rule.

These are estimates. Confirm the quantities with your supplier before you order.

<h2 id="faq">Frequently asked questions</h2>

### How much mulch do I need?

Multiply length × width × depth, with the depth in feet, then add the extra. A bed 20 ft by 4 ft and 3 in deep holds 20 cubic feet. With 5 percent extra that is 21 cubic feet, or 0.78 cubic yards: 11 bags of 2 cu ft.

### How many bags of mulch are in a yard?

A cubic yard is exactly 27 cubic feet, so it takes 13.5 bags of 2 cu ft or 9 bags of 3 cu ft. Round up to whole bags when you buy.

### How deep should mulch be?

A layer of 2 to 3 inches is common for flower beds and around shrubs. A thinner layer lets weeds through, and a much thicker one can keep water and air from the soil. Follow your local practice and the advice of your supplier.

### How much area does a yard of mulch cover?

One cubic yard covers about 162 square feet at 2 inches deep, 108 square feet at 3 inches and 81 square feet at 4 inches, before any extra. The calculator works it out for your exact bed.

### How much does a bag of mulch cover?

A 2 cu ft bag covers about 12 square feet at 2 inches deep and 8 square feet at 3 inches. A 3 cu ft bag covers about 18 square feet at 2 inches and 12 square feet at 3 inches.

### Is mulch cheaper in bags or in bulk?

It depends on the size of the job and the delivery fee. Type the price per bag and the price per cubic yard, and the calculator shows both costs. For the example bed, 7 bags of 3 cu ft at $5 cost $35.00, and bulk at $36 a yard costs $28.00 before delivery.

### How much extra mulch should I add?

The calculator starts at 5 percent, which covers settling and the mulch lost while spreading. Add more for a new bed on loose soil. Removing the extra from the example drops the order from 11 bags of 2 cu ft to 10.

### Can I use meters and centimeters?

Yes. Press Metric (m, cm), then type the length and the width in meters and the depth in centimeters. The result leads with cubic meters. Switching units never changes your numbers, only how they are written.
