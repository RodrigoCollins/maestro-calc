# How to calculate cubic yards

Cubic yards are length × width × depth in feet, divided by 27: a slab 10 ft by 10 ft and 4 in thick is 33.33 cubic feet, or 1.23 cubic yards. Concrete, gravel, topsoil and mulch are all ordered by the cubic yard, and the math is the same for each: measure in feet, multiply, divide.

## Use the formula

Cubic yards = length × width × depth ÷ 27, with all three measured in feet.

A cubic yard is a cube 3 ft on each side, and 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 cubic feet. Divide cubic feet by 27 to get cubic yards; multiply cubic yards by 27 to get the cubic feet back.

## Turn inches into feet

The depth or the thickness is usually measured in inches: divide it by 12. When the decimal does not end, multiply by the fraction instead:

| Inches | Feet    |
| ------ | ------- |
| 1 in   | 1/12 ft |
| 2 in   | 1/6 ft  |
| 3 in   | 1/4 ft  |
| 4 in   | 1/3 ft  |
| 6 in   | 1/2 ft  |
| 12 in  | 1 ft    |

Rounding 4 in to 0.33 ft comes up short: 10 × 10 × 0.33 = 33 cubic feet, where 10 × 10 × 1/3 = 33.33.

## A concrete slab

A slab 10 ft by 10 ft, 4 in thick:

- 4 in is 1/3 ft, so the slab is 10 × 10 × 1/3 = 33.33 cubic feet.
- 33.33 ÷ 27 = 1.23 cubic yards.
- With the 10 percent extra the [concrete calculator](/concrete-calculator) adds by default, that is 36.67 cubic feet, or 1.36 cubic yards.

The page [How many bags of concrete for a 10x10 slab?](/concrete/10x10-slab) gives the same slab at 4, 5 and 6 in thick, in cubic yards and in bags.

## A gravel driveway

A layer 60 ft long and 10 ft wide, 4 in deep, is 60 × 10 × 1/3 = 200 cubic feet, and 200 ÷ 27 = 7.41 cubic yards in place. Gravel packs down when it is compacted, and it is often sold by the ton, so the [gravel calculator](/gravel-calculator) turns that volume into tons and into the loose cubic yards to order. The guide [How much gravel for a driveway?](/guides/how-much-gravel-for-a-driveway) explains the layers.

## Topsoil for a raised bed

A raised bed 8 ft by 4 ft, filled 12 in deep, holds 8 × 4 × 1 = 32.00 cubic feet: 32 ÷ 27 = 1.19 cubic yards. The [topsoil calculator](/topsoil-calculator) adds the extra and counts the bags; the guide [How much soil for a raised bed?](/guides/how-much-soil-for-a-raised-bed) covers the depth and the mix.

## Mulch for a bed

A bed 20 ft by 4 ft, mulched 3 in deep, takes 20 × 4 × 1/4 = 20 cubic feet: 20 ÷ 27 = 0.74 cubic yards. The [mulch calculator](/mulch-calculator) adds the extra and counts the bags; the guide [How much mulch do I need?](/guides/how-much-mulch-do-i-need) covers the depth and the bag sizes.

## Circles: columns, tubes and round beds

A circle's area is π × radius × radius, and the radius is half the diameter. Multiply the area by the height or the depth:

- A column 12 in across and 8 ft tall has a radius of 1/2 ft: π × 1/2 × 1/2 × 8 = 6.28 cubic feet, and 6.28 ÷ 27 = 0.23 cubic yards. With the concrete calculator's 10 percent extra, that is 0.26 cubic yards.
- A round bed 6 ft across, mulched 3 in deep, has a radius of 3 ft: π × 3 × 3 × 1/4 = 7.07 cubic feet, or 0.26 cubic yards.

## Odd shapes

Split the area into rectangles and circles, work out the cubic feet of each, add them, and divide the total by 27 once. Dividing at the end keeps the rounding to one step.

Then round your order up, not down: a little left over costs less than a second trip or a second delivery. The gravel calculator below works out a layer in cubic yards and tons; each calculator linked above does the same for its material.
