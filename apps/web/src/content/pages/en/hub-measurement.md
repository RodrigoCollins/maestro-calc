# Measurement tools

Most mistakes on a job start with a measurement. These calculators read lengths the way a tape measure does, in feet, inches and fractions: one adds, subtracts, multiplies or divides them, one turns a length and a width into square feet, square yards and square meters, and one gives the board feet of lumber from its thickness, width and length.

A foot is exactly 0.3048 meters, so a square foot is exactly 0.09290304 square meters. The guides show how to read every mark of a tape and how to find the area of a room that is not a rectangle.

## Measure without the common mistakes

- **Read the tape to the smallest mark you trust.** Most tapes mark sixteenths of an inch; [read a tape measure and add fractions](/guides/read-a-tape-measure-and-add-fractions) shows every mark.
- **Convert feet and inches with care.** 12 ft 6 in is 12.5 ft, not 12.6 ft. Type the length as you read it and let the calculator convert it, or use the [decimal feet to feet and inches chart](/measurement/decimal-feet-to-feet-and-inches-chart).
- **Measure each side twice and use the longer reading.** Rooms are rarely square, and the shorter reading leaves you short of material.
- **Count lumber on the size in its name.** A board foot is 1 in by 12 in by 1 ft, and the American Softwood Lumber Standard (PS 20-20) counts it on the nominal size: a 2x4x8 is 5.33 board feet even though a dry 2x4 measures 1 1/2 by 3 1/2 in. The [board feet calculator](/board-feet-calculator) shows both.
- **Split an L-shaped room into rectangles** that do not overlap, find each area and add them. [How to calculate square footage](/guides/how-to-calculate-square-footage) works one out.

The conversions are exact, from NIST Special Publication 811: 1 in is 2.54 cm, 1 ft is 0.3048 m, 1 sq ft is 0.09290304 sq m, and 1 sq yd is 9 sq ft. A 10 × 12 ft room is 120 sq ft, 13.33 sq yd or 11.15 sq m: [the 10 × 12 ft page](/measurement/10x12-square-feet) works it out in both systems. The [construction glossary](/construction-glossary) gives every term in English and Spanish.
