# How much soil for a raised bed?

A raised bed 8 ft by 4 ft, filled 12 in deep, holds 32 cubic feet of soil: 35.20 cubic feet, or 1.30 cubic yards, with 10 percent extra for settling. How much you buy is the size of the box; what you fill it with depends on what is under the bed and what you will grow.

## Decide how deep to fill it

The depth depends on the roots of what you grow. For a bed on pavement or on hard, compacted soil, the University of Maryland Extension gives at least 8 inches for leafy greens, beans and cucumbers, and 12 to 24 inches for peppers, tomatoes and squash. On loose garden soil, the roots can also go down into the ground below the bed.

## Choose the mix

Fill the bed with a light, well-drained mix, not with garden soil alone. Two university mixes:

- **On a lawn or on soil:** compost and topsoil, 1 part compost to 2 parts topsoil, or equal parts (University of Maryland Extension).
- **Equal thirds:** topsoil, organic matter (compost, well-rotted manure or peat) and coarse sand, or about 70 percent topsoil and 30 percent compost (Iowa State University Extension).

On pavement, the University of Maryland Extension fills with compost and a soilless growing mix in equal parts, with topsoil up to a fifth of the volume only in beds at least 16 inches deep.

## Work out the volume

Say the bed is 8 ft long and 4 ft wide, filled 12 in deep:

- 12 in is 1 ft, so the bed holds 8 × 4 × 1 = 32 cubic feet.
- With the topsoil calculator's 10 percent extra for settling, that is 35.20 cubic feet, or 1.30 cubic yards.
- At 1 part compost to 2 parts topsoil, the topsoil is 2/3 of it, 23.47 cubic feet, and the compost 1/3, 11.73 cubic feet.
- In bags of 1.5 cubic feet: 23.47 ÷ 1.5 = 15.6, so 16 bags of topsoil, and 11.73 ÷ 1.5 = 7.8, so 8 bags of compost.

For several beds of the same size, multiply. Past a cubic yard or so, compare the bags with a bulk delivery of a ready mix.

## Fill it in layers

Do not dump the whole mix in at once. Iowa State University Extension advises adding a few inches, working it into the soil below, and repeating until the bed is full, so no layer stops the water or the roots.

The topsoil calculator below works out the cubic yards and the bags for the whole bed. Split the result by your mix to know how much of each to buy.
