# How much mulch do I need?

A bed 20 ft by 4 ft, mulched 3 in deep, takes 20.00 cubic feet of mulch: 21.00 with 5 percent extra, or 11 bags of 2 cubic feet. Mulch is sold by volume, in bags marked with the cubic feet they hold or in bulk by the cubic yard, so measure the bed, choose the depth and multiply.

## Measure the bed

Measure the length and the width of the bed in feet. A bed that is not a rectangle splits into rectangles: work out each one and add them. Leave out what the mulch does not cover, such as a patio or a large shrub's trunk.

## Choose the depth

Clemson University Cooperative Extension advises keeping a layer of organic mulch 2 to 3 inches deep over the roots of trees and shrubs and in plant beds, with at least a hand's width of space between the mulch and the trunk or stem. The University of Florida IFAS Florida-Friendly Landscaping program also gives 2 to 3 inches, says to replenish the mulch to keep that depth, and to keep it 12 to 18 inches from the base of trees, never piled against the trunk.

On a bed that still has some mulch, measure what is left and buy only the difference: a bed with 1 in left needs 2 in more to reach 3 in.

## Turn the depth into feet

The volume is length × width × depth, all in feet, so divide the inches by 12: 2 in is 1/6 ft, 3 in is 1/4 ft and 4 in is 1/3 ft. Multiply by the fraction rather than a rounded decimal, so the result does not come up short.

## Work out the cubic feet and cubic yards

Say the bed is 20 ft long and 4 ft wide, mulched 3 in deep:

- 3 in is 1/4 ft, so the bed takes 20 × 4 × 1/4 = 20.00 cubic feet.
- With the mulch calculator's 5 percent extra, that is 21.00 cubic feet.
- One cubic yard is 27 cubic feet, a cube 3 ft on each side, so 21 ÷ 27 = 0.78 cubic yards.

The same bed at 2 in takes 20 × 4 × 1/6 = 13.33 cubic feet, or 14.00 cubic feet with the extra: 0.52 cubic yards.

## Count the bags

A bag holds the cubic feet printed on it, most often 2 or 3. Divide the volume by the bag size and round up to a whole bag:

- 21.00 ÷ 2 = 10.5, so 11 bags of 2 cubic feet.
- 21.00 ÷ 3 = 7, so 7 bags of 3 cubic feet.
- At 2 in, 14.00 ÷ 2 = 7, so 7 bags of 2 cubic feet.

## Compare bags with bulk

The bigger the bed, the more bags it takes. A bed 30 ft by 10 ft at 3 in takes 30 × 10 × 1/4 = 75.00 cubic feet, or 78.75 with the extra: 2.92 cubic yards in bulk, or 40 bags of 2 cubic feet. Ask the supplier for the price per cubic yard, any minimum order and the delivery charge, and compare that with the price of the bags.
