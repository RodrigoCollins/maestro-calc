# How to estimate and quote a drywall job

A drywall quote is two lists: what you buy and what you do. The first comes from the size of the room. The second comes mostly from the level of finish the client expects, so agree on it before you price anything.

## Measure the room

Measure the length and the width of the room at the floor and the height of the walls. The walls are 2 × (length + width) × height. Take out the doors and the windows, and add length × width when you are hanging the ceiling too.

A bedroom 12 ft by 12 ft with 8 ft walls has 2 × (12 + 12) × 8 = 384 square feet of walls. Leaving out about 20 square feet for its door and about 15 for each of two windows, 334 remain, and the ceiling adds 144: 478 square feet to cover.

## Count the materials

- **Sheets.** Add the extra for cuts, 10 percent to start, and divide by the area of one sheet: 32 square feet for 4 × 8, 48 for 4 × 12. The bedroom takes 478 × 1.1 ÷ 32 = 16.4, so 17 sheets of 4 × 8, or 11 sheets of 4 × 12. Fewer, longer sheets also mean fewer joints to finish.
- **Joint compound.** About 9 gallons per 1,000 square feet for a standard finish, from USG's figure of two 4.5-gallon pails for 1,000 square feet. The bedroom takes about 4.30 gallons: 1 pail. A higher level of finish takes more.
- **Joint tape.** About 370 feet of paper tape per 1,000 square feet, the coverage on USG's data sheet. The bedroom takes about 176.86 feet: one 500 ft roll.
- **What you count on site.** Screws, corner bead and trim depend on the framing, the corners and the code where you build. Count the outside corners and the trim on site, and follow the panel maker's fastening schedule for the screws.

## Agree on the level of finish

The Gypsum Association's GA-214 sets six levels of finish. Each level adds coats of joint compound, so it is the biggest difference in labor between two quotes for the same room:

| Level | Joints                                          | Fasteners | Typically for                                               |
| ----- | ----------------------------------------------- | --------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0     | No taping                                       | None      | Temporary work, or when the final decoration is not decided |
| 1     | Tape set in compound                            | None      | Areas out of view: above ceilings, attics                   |
| 2     | Tape with a thin coat over it                   | 1 coat    | A base for tile, garages, storage                           |
| 3     | 1 more coat on flat joints                      | 2 coats   | Heavy or medium texture, heavy wallcoverings                |
| 4     | 2 more coats on flat joints                     | 3 coats   | Smooth walls with flat paint or light texture               |
| 5     | Level 4 plus a skim coat over the whole surface | 3 coats   | Gloss or dark paint, or strong light across the wall        |

Interior corners get one more coat from level 3 up, and corner bead and other accessories get 1, 2 or 3 coats at levels 2, 3 and 4 or 5. Write the level in the quote: "Level 4 finish, ready for paint" is something the client can check; "smooth finish" is not.

## Put the quote together

1. **Materials:** each line of the list above times your supplier's price. The calculator totals the sheets for you when you enter the price per sheet.
2. **Labor:** hanging, then taping and each coat of the level you agreed, then sanding. Price it the way you work, by the sheet, by the square foot or by the day, and keep the same method from job to job so you can compare.
3. **What is in and what is not:** the ceiling, the level of finish, primer and paint, moving furniture, haul-off of scraps, and any special panels the code asks for, such as moisture-resistant board in a bathroom.

These are estimates. Confirm the quantities with your supplier before you order. The drywall calculator below does the measuring and the counting for any room size.
