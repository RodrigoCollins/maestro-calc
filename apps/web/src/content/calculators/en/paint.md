<h2 id="how-to-use">How to use the paint calculator</h2>

The calculator tells you how many gallons of paint the walls of a room need, and how many gallon cans to buy. The result updates as you type, so there is nothing to submit.

1. Measure the length and the width of the room at the floor, and the height of the walls from the floor to the ceiling. Type them the way you read a tape: 12 ft, 12 ft 6 in or 8 ft 1 1/2 in. A plain number is read in feet.
2. Count the doors and the windows. The calculator leaves out about 20 square feet for each door and about 15 square feet for each window.
3. Choose the number of coats. Two coats is the usual choice for a new color; one coat can do for a fresh coat of the same color.
4. To see the cost, type the price per gallon your store charges.

The result leads with the gallons of paint. It also shows the same amount in liters, the area to paint, the wall area before the doors and windows come out, and the number of gallon cans to buy.

To work in metric, press Metric (m, cm). Lengths are then read in meters, and the result leads with liters. Switching units never changes your numbers, only how they are written. Use Copy link to send your exact numbers to a partner, a client or the store, and the Español link to open the same numbers in Spanish.

<h2 id="formulas">Formulas and worked example</h2>

The calculator works from the size of the room to the cans you buy:

- Wall area = 2 × (length + width) × height.
- Area to paint = wall area − 20 square feet per door − 15 square feet per window, never below zero.
- Gallons = area to paint × coats ÷ coverage per gallon.
- Cans = gallons, rounded up to a whole gallon.

It uses approximately 350 square feet per gallon for one coat, the low end of the 350 to 400 square feet per gallon that a Sherwin-Williams data sheet gives for an interior latex paint. Using the low end means the estimate does not come up short. A gallon is exactly 231 cubic inches, or 3.785411784 liters (NIST SP 811).

Worked example: a bedroom 12 ft by 12 ft with 8 ft walls, one door, two windows and two coats.

- The wall area is 2 × (12 + 12) × 8 = 384.00 square feet.
- One door and two windows leave out 20 + 15 + 15 = 50 square feet, so the area to paint is 334.00 square feet.
- Two coats cover 668 square feet, and 668 ÷ 350 = 1.91 gallons, which is 7.22 liters.
- You buy 2 gallon cans.
- At $40 a gallon, the paint costs $80.00.

The calculator keeps exact fractions from the first input to the last step. The can count is rounded up, and every other figure is rounded only when it is shown. The page [How we calculate](/methodology#paint) lists the formulas and the cases we test.

<h2 id="tips">Tips and common mistakes</h2>

Measure the walls, not the floor. A room that is 12 ft by 12 ft has 144 square feet of floor but 384 square feet of wall at 8 ft high, so a floor measurement badly undercounts the paint.

Check the label of your paint. Coverage changes with the product, the color and the surface. Rough or bare surfaces such as new drywall, stucco or block soak up more paint, so plan on less coverage there, or a coat of primer first.

Large openings are not standard. For a double door, a wide window or an open archway, count it as two or three openings, or leave the wall area as it is and keep the paint as a margin.

Buy the paint for the whole job at once when you can. Cans mixed at different times can differ a little in color, so a second trip may not match the first.

Keep a little paint for touch-ups. A partly used can stored tightly closed is useful when a wall gets a scuff months later.

The calculator covers the walls only. Ceilings, trim and doors take their own paint, often a different product, so estimate them apart.

These are estimates. Confirm the quantities with your store before you buy.

<h2 id="faq">Frequently asked questions</h2>

### How much paint do I need for a room?

Multiply the perimeter of the room by the wall height, take out the doors and windows, multiply by the coats and divide by about 350 square feet per gallon. A 12 ft by 12 ft room with 8 ft walls, one door and two windows needs about 1.91 gallons for two coats: 2 gallon cans.

### How many square feet does a gallon of paint cover?

About 350 to 400 square feet for one coat on a smooth, primed wall, according to the data sheet of a common interior latex paint. The calculator uses 350, the low end. Rough or bare surfaces take more paint.

### How many coats of paint do I need?

Two coats is the usual choice, and it is what the calculator starts with. A big change of color, a dark color or a bare wall may need more. One coat can be enough for the same color over a sound finish.

### Should I subtract doors and windows?

Yes, for a closer estimate. The calculator takes out about 20 square feet for each door and 15 for each window. Without any openings, the example room would need 2.19 gallons instead of 1.91.

### How much paint for a 12 by 12 room?

With 8 ft walls, one door, two windows and two coats, about 1.91 gallons, so 2 gallon cans. With three coats it is 2.86 gallons, so 3 cans.

### Should I buy gallons or a 5-gallon bucket?

The calculator counts gallon cans. When the job needs about five gallons or more, a 5-gallon bucket of the same paint is often cheaper per gallon. Compare the prices at your store.

### Does the calculator include the ceiling?

No. It covers the walls only. Ceilings and trim usually take a different paint, so estimate them apart. For a ceiling, its area is the length times the width of the room.

### Can I use meters?

Yes. Press Metric (m, cm), then type the length, the width and the wall height in meters. The result leads with liters, and the cans are still gallon cans. Switching units never changes your numbers, only how they are written.
