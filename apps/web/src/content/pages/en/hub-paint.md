# Painting tools

Work out the paint for the walls of a room before you buy: the gallons for the coats you choose, with the doors and windows left out, and the whole gallon cans to buy. The calculator also totals the cost when you enter your price per gallon.

The first guide goes further: the supplies and the prep that change a price the most, the steps of the work, and how to write a quote your client can check. The second gives the square feet one gallon covers in one, two and three coats, and the gallons for a room.

## Plan a painting job in four steps

1. **Measure the walls, not the floor.** The wall area is the distance around the room times the height: a 12 × 12 ft room with 8 ft walls has 384 sq ft of wall and only 144 sq ft of floor.
2. **Take off the openings.** The [paint calculator](/paint-calculator) counts about 20 sq ft for a door and 15 sq ft for a window. A double door or a wide opening counts as two or three.
3. **Divide by the coverage.** The manufacturer's data sheet the calculator uses gives about 350 to 400 sq ft per gallon in one coat; the calculator takes the low end, so two coats come to about 175 sq ft per gallon. [How much does a gallon of paint cover?](/guides/how-much-does-a-gallon-of-paint-cover) has the table for one, two and three coats.
4. **Round up to whole cans and buy them together.** Mix the cans in one bucket before you start, so two batches match on the wall, and keep a closed can for touch-ups.

New drywall, bare plaster and a strong change of color take more paint, or a primer first. The ceiling, the trim and the doors take their own paint. The room pages work common sizes out at 8, 9 and 10 ft, for example the [paint for a 12 × 12 ft room](/painting/paint-for-12x12-room).
